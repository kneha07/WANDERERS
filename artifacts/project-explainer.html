WANDERERS

WANDERERS finds objects that move or change in the infrared sky. It then uses the 102 colours of NASA SPHEREx to show what each object is likely to be.

It flips through real images from 2010 to 2026, the way Pluto was found by flipping two photos. Every claim shows its evidence on screen.

Rule: candidate, never discovery
Live site Explorer: WISE 0855 Sky Survey Repo
Why it exists

Objectives

  • Meet all six challenge requirements

    Data sync, detector switch, quality flags, citizen votes, candidate ranking and a billion-object search. Each one is live and tested.

  • See motion, then read composition

    A flipbook shows that it moved. The 0.75–5.0 µm spectrum shows what it is: water, methane, CO and CO₂ bands. A usable spectrum exists for 5 of the 11 shipped targets.

  • Show the evidence, not a score

    Stated rules turn measurements into a class and a confidence, for example "3 of 3 key checks support it". The full list of checks prints under the headline. No black box.

  • Never over-claim

    Anything that Gaia or a catalogue does not explain is a candidate. Each card has a "What this view cannot show" box.

  • Use only real, public data

    SPHEREx, WISE, NEOWISE, Gaia DR3, JWST, Kepler. Nothing is simulated. AI writes text only, never measurements.

  • Open with no install

    The site is static and the data is committed. A clone or the live link just works, also on a phone.

The core logic

How the evidence card decides

pipeline/evidence.py asks these questions in order. The first "yes" wins. Confidence counts the key checks for the chosen class: high when all pass (at least 2) or 3 or more pass, medium when 2 pass, low otherwise. It drops one step, with the reason shown, when most SPHEREx points are saturated. Read the printed list of checks, not only the headline.

Cold and red, and it moved?Cold brown dwarf
Brightness varies between surveys?Variable star
Moved, and Gaia knows it?Nearby star, fast proper motion
Moved, and matches a published motion?Nearby object, published motion
Moved, with no match?Unexplained mover → candidate
Listed as variable, but quiet here?Known variable, quiet in these snapshots
None of the aboveSteady star

Known limits

  • Bright stars saturate SPHEREx. Those points are dropped, and the card says so.
  • A usable spectrum exists for 5 of the 11 shipped targets. Bright stars such as Barnard's Star have none, and the panel says so.
  • Aperture photometry gives the shape of a spectrum, not precise absolute flux.
  • One Star Fix photo is right to about 50 to 150 km. It needs the local server with the plate solver installed.
  • The public site shows only prepared targets. New coordinates need node server.js (about 12–15 minutes).
  • The field search recovers its own target in 8 of 9 moving-target fields. WISE 0855 is still missed.
  • An empty "what else moved" list means "nothing found", not "nothing there".
